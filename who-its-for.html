Who it’s for

Built around real jobs.

nook is designed for the people who actually run a hostel or PG, and for the residents they look after.

The journey

From first look to move-out.

Every step is designed with its failure case in mind, because that’s where software usually lets people down.

  1. 1

    Discover Later

    A public visitor opens a property and sees only published details, never anything about residents.

  2. 2

    Explore the layout Later

    Switch floors and see which beds are free and where they are.

  3. 3

    Enquire Later

    Leave contact details, with phone verification if enabled. If verification fails the enquiry stays unverified and no bed is held.

  4. 4

    Choose a bed Later

    Pick a specific bed. If it’s taken first, the person is told plainly and sent back to the map, never silently reassigned.

  5. 5

    Approval

    Staff review and decide. A rejection records a reason and creates no stay or charge.

  6. 6

    Allocation

    The bed is committed atomically, with availability re-checked. A conflict is rejected with current state.

  7. 7

    Check-in

    Move-in is recorded and the rent terms are attached to the stay.

  8. 8

    Living there

    Charges and payments accrue. Arrears are surfaced for follow-up.

  9. 9

    Check-out

    Leaving is recorded first. Financial settlement is a separate step.

A day in the life

What each role actually does.

Property manager

Admission and allocation.

  1. Open the enquiry queue and review each applicant against the property’s criteria
  2. Confirm or change the requested bed, with the map showing live state
  3. Approve. nook validates the resident, the bed and the stage change in one transaction
  4. Record move-in. The stay becomes active

If something goes wrong: someone allocated the bed seconds earlier, so the action is rejected and current state is shown instead of overwriting.

Finance clerk

Recording a payment.

  1. Open the resident and see outstanding charges, oldest first
  2. Enter the amount, date, method and reference (UPI id, bank reference or a cash receipt number)
  3. Choose which charge the payment settles
  4. Save. The payment and its allocation commit together, or not at all
  5. Hand over the numbered receipt

If something goes wrong: a duplicate reference is refused and linked to the original, and a retried request returns the first result instead of recording twice.

Owner

The daily question: who owes money?

  1. Open the dashboard for outstanding dues by resident, with ageing
  2. See today’s collections by method, plus current occupancy
  3. Drill into a resident and read the statement line by line, summing to the balance

If something goes wrong: if a number looks wrong and can’t be explained, trust is lost, which is why the statement is a screen in its own right, not an export.

Warden / front desk

Knowing who is where.

  1. Look up which resident is in which bed
  2. Log a maintenance request
  3. Get on with the day, with no balances, payment references or documents in view

If something goes wrong: if a warden needs to see money at one property, that’s a deliberate grant by the owner, never a default.

nook support

Helping a customer.

  1. Ask for access with a stated reason
  2. Get time-limited access, if granted
  3. Every action is audited and visible to the customer

If something goes wrong: there is no standing login. Until a support console exists, support is a conversation.

Who can do what

A starting baseline for access.

Roles are bundles of permissions, and the property owner decides the final grants.

CapabilityOwnerManagerFinanceWarden
Organisation settings and membersYesNoNoNo
Resident profilesYesScopedScopedScoped
Rooms, beds and assignmentsYesYesReadScoped
Stays and check-outYesYesNoScoped
Balances and reportsYesScopedYesNo
Record a paymentYesNoYesNo
Export dataYesNoGrantNo
View the audit trailYesScopedGrantNo

Scoped means only for assigned properties or with an explicit grant. Grant means the owner must grant it, so it doesn’t come with the role.

Three separations nook never collapses

Record ≠ confirm

Recording that money arrived is not the authority to attest that it did.

Profile ≠ documents

Being able to read a profile never implies being able to read identity documents.

View ≠ export

Seeing a balance on screen is not permission to take the data out of the system.
