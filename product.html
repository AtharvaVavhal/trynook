01 · Property

Property & inventory

Everything starts with what physically exists: buildings, floors, rooms and individual beds. Nothing else can be correct until this is.

Set up by the owner, without a developer

  1. Create the organisation and owner account. No public self-service sign-up during the pilot.
  2. Add a property: name, address, timezone and the settings the hostel actually uses.
  3. Define buildings and floors, in order, so “above” and “below” mean something.
  4. Create rooms using the labels the property already uses.
  5. Add and label beds inside each room.
  6. Position beds on a floor layout (optional) to power the interactive map.
  7. Invite staff and grant permissions per property.
Your vocabulary, not ours. If your hostel calls a room “Annexe 2”, nook calls it “Annexe 2”. Renaming inventory to fit a tidy scheme means staff keep a mental translation table, and it fails at the worst moment.

Five bed states, kept separate from occupancy

Whether a bed is usable and whether someone lives in it are different questions. Mixing them makes “under maintenance” look like “occupied” and spoils your occupancy reports.

StateMeaningCan be allocated?
AvailableUsable and unoccupied for the period in questionYes
OccupiedAn active assignment covers the periodNo
BlockedDeliberately withheld: staff room, storage, held for a returning residentNo
Under maintenanceTemporarily unusableNo
ReservedHeld pending a decision, if the owner chooses to allow holdsOnly if enabled

Occupancy is derived

It comes from dated assignments, never from a flag on the bed, so a late checkout can’t leave the record quietly wrong.

Changes are conservative

nook refuses to delete or block a bed someone is living in, and names the exact records in the way.

History never disappears

A bed that has ever been used is deactivated, not deleted, so old receipts still show the right label.

Every inventory change records who made it, when, and the before and after. That makes “who was in bed 203C last March?” answerable, which matters in deposit disputes and verification enquiries.

Several properties? Adding another needs no developer. Owners see reports across properties, while staff stay scoped to the properties they’re assigned.

02 · Admissions

Admission & allocation

From first enquiry to a committed bed, and on into the stay.

  1. Enquiry
  2. Review
  3. Approved
  4. Allocated
  5. Checked in
  6. Active stay
  7. Notice given
  8. Checkout
People decide who is admitted. Many hostels approve residents by hand, and that’s a screening decision, not paperwork. nook reproduces that: an enquiry reaches staff, staff decide, and only then is a bed allocated. Instant allotment stays off unless the owner approves it.

Choosing a bed doesn’t reserve it

The map shows availability at the moment you look. A choice travels with the enquiry as a preference, and if the bed is taken before approval, the person is told plainly and offered comparable alternatives.

Approval is atomic, so double-booking can’t happen

  1. Staff review the enquiry and see live availability, not what it was when the enquiry came in.
  2. The server locks the bed inside a single transaction.
  3. Availability is re-checked against current assignments, never against anything the browser sent.
  4. Everything is verified: the resident, the bed, the organisation and that the stage change is allowed.
  5. One write records the assignment, updates the bed and moves the stay forward together, or none of it happens.

If two staff approve the same bed at the same moment, exactly one succeeds and the other is told why.

03 · People

Residents & stays

A person, their stays and their bed history are held separately, so none overwrites another.

RecordWhat it holdsHow long it lasts
ResidentThe person: name, contact details and only the fields a workflow really needsAcross stays; retained after departure per policy
StayOne admission to one departure: property, dates, state and the rent termsOne stay
AssignmentWhich bed, over which dates, who recorded it and whyAppend-only; one per room change
Lifecycle eventEach transition with who and whenPermanent

Returning residents

Recognised, not duplicated, and their earlier history stays intact.

Room moves

Recorded as new assignments, so every past charge and receipt keeps its context.

Collect less

Every field has a cost, so nook collects only what a named workflow uses.

04 · Money

Finance

The part of nook designed to answer one question: “who owes what, and why?”

nook records money. It does not hold money. Residents pay the property directly by cash, UPI or bank transfer. nook records what arrived and reconciles it against what was owed. It operates no wallet and processes no payments.

Six records, one derived number

ChargeA claim for a billing period. The amount is frozen when issued.
PaymentMoney that arrived, with its method and reference.
AllocationLinks one payment to one charge, for an amount.
AdjustmentA signed correction with a reason and an approver.
ReversalNegates a posted record. The original stays visible.
DepositA liability held separately. Never mixed into rent.
Outstanding balance=charges + debit adjustments−allocations−credit adjustmentscomputed every time, never stored as truth

Five rules that keep the books honest

  1. Money is stored as whole paise, never as floating-point numbers, so rounding errors can’t creep in.
  2. Nothing posted is edited or deleted. A correction is a new record that points back at the old one.
  3. Balances are computed from the records, and any mismatch raises an alert instead of being quietly patched.
  4. Allocations are limited on both sides: a payment can’t be spread beyond what it was, nor a charge paid beyond what it was.
  5. Duplicates are blocked by the database itself, so a retried request can’t record the same payment twice.

Billing follows each resident

A resident admitted on the 17th is billed on the 17th, not on a month-end batch.

Deposits stay out of revenue

A deposit is a liability with its own life cycle, kept out of every collection figure by design.

Receipts for every payment

Cash, UPI and bank transfers are recorded against the right charge, each with a receipt.

05 · Access

Roles & permissions

Everyone sees what their job needs, and nothing more.

RoleNeeds from nook
Owner / partnerOccupancy and dues at a glance, financial reports partners can agree on, control over who can do what
Property managerAdmissions, bed allocation, resident lifecycle and daily follow-up on arrears
Finance clerk / cashierRecord each payment against the right charge, issue receipts, reconcile cash and transfers
Warden / front deskWho is in which bed and day-to-day tasks, with no financial or document access by default

Access is granted per property, not per organisation: a manager responsible for one of three properties sees one. Permissions are checked on the server, and nobody can grant a permission they don’t hold themselves.

06 · Day to day

Operations After the pilot

Deliberately light. nook is a hostel management product, not a maintenance product.

Maintenance requests

Log what’s broken, who’s on it and whether it’s fixed. Recurring faults per room become visible.

Complaints

Service feedback such as noise or cleanliness, visible to operational staff.

Reports

Open requests by age and category, resolution times and repeat issues, all from the source records.

Sensitive incident records will only be built together with a proper access model, because a free-text box with no restrictions is worse than none.
